Sample Questions and Answers

Year 1 Batch 24: Sample Questions with Answers

This document contains the questions from the uploaded Year 1 Batch 24 sample paper, with answers and a short explanation for each. Section A answers are the True/False marks given in the sample paper. Section B answers were worked out from the questions. Use this for revision; if you use any wording in assessed work, put it in your own words.

Section A: True or False (30 questions)

1. A credit purchase of inventory increases both inventory and accounts payable.

Answer: True. Inventory (asset) rises and accounts payable (liability) rises.

2. Management accounting reports must always follow the same format as published financial statements.

Answer: False. Management accounting has no set statutory format; it is designed for internal needs.

3. Total fixed costs remain constant within the relevant range of activity.

Answer: True. Total fixed costs stay flat within the relevant range; only fixed cost per unit changes.

4. Financial accounting information is prepared exclusively for internal managers.

Answer: False. Financial accounting is mainly for external users such as investors and creditors.

5. Every transaction recorded under the double-entry system affects at least two accounts.

Answer: True. Every double-entry transaction has at least one debit and one credit.

6. Drawings by the owner are recorded as operating expenses in the income statement.

Answer: False. Drawings reduce owner's capital; they are not an expense of the business.

7. Financial accounting information is generally prepared according to applicable accounting standards and regulations.

Answer: True. Financial statements follow accounting standards and regulations.

8. The break-even point is the level of sales at which total revenue equals total costs.

Answer: True. At break-even, total revenue equals total costs and profit is zero.

9. Contribution is calculated by subtracting fixed costs from sales revenue.

Answer: False. Contribution = sales revenue minus variable costs, not fixed costs.

10. Standard costing compares actual costs with predetermined standard costs.

Answer: True. Standard costing compares actual results with predetermined standards and analyses the variances.

11. Accumulated depreciation represents the total depreciation charged on an asset since its acquisition, subject to relevant adjustments.

Answer: True. It is the total depreciation charged on the asset since acquisition, less adjustments such as disposals.

12. Equipment purchased for cash must always be recorded as an immediate expense in the income statement.

Answer: False. Equipment is capitalised as a non-current asset and then depreciated, not expensed at once.

13. The margin of safety measures the difference between actual or budgeted sales and break-even sales.

Answer: True. Margin of safety = actual or budgeted sales minus break-even sales.

14. Opportunity cost is the total amount of money already spent on a business activity.

Answer: False. Money already spent is sunk cost; opportunity cost is the benefit given up from the next best alternative.

15. The payback period measures the time required to recover the initial investment from a project's cash inflows.

Answer: True. Payback period is the time needed for cash inflows to cover the initial investment.

16. Management accounting reports can include both financial and non-financial information.

Answer: True. They may include non-financial measures such as customer satisfaction.

17. Bad debts are amounts owed by customers that are considered uncollectible.

Answer: True. Bad debts are receivables that will not be collected and are written off.

18. Prepaid expenses represent amounts that a business has received in advance from its customers.

Answer: False. Prepaid expenses are paid in advance by the business; amounts received in advance from customers are deferred income.

19. Ratio analysis can help investors and creditors evaluate a company's financial performance and position.

Answer: True. Ratios help investors and creditors judge performance, liquidity and position.

20. A budget is a report that records only the actual financial results of a completed period.

Answer: False. A budget is a forward plan of expected results, not a record of actual results.

21. A trial balance checks whether total debit balances equal total credit balances in the ledger accounts.

Answer: True. A trial balance lists ledger balances to check total debits equal total credits.

22. Purchasing equipment on credit decreases both total assets and total liabilities.

Answer: False. Buying equipment on credit increases both assets (equipment) and liabilities (payable).

23. When the owner invests cash into a business, both assets and owner's capital increase.

Answer: True. Cash (asset) increases and capital increases when the owner invests.

24. Paying a supplier in cash increases both cash and accounts payable.

Answer: False. Paying a supplier in cash decreases both cash and accounts payable.

25. Trade discount is usually deducted from the list price before the transaction is recorded in the accounting books.

Answer: True. Trade discount is taken off the list price before the sale or purchase is recorded.

26. Cash discount is offered to encourage customers to delay payment of their accounts.

Answer: False. Cash discount encourages early payment, not delayed payment.

27. The income statement reports revenue and expenses over a specific accounting period.

Answer: True. The income statement covers revenue and expenses for a period of time.

28. The statement of financial position reports the financial position of a business over an entire accounting period.

Answer: False. The statement of financial position shows position at a specific date, not over a period.

29. Working capital is calculated by subtracting current assets from current liabilities.

Answer: False. Working capital = current assets minus current liabilities, not the other way round.

30. A positive net present value (NPV) generally indicates that an investment is expected to add value when evaluated using the specified discount rate.

Answer: True. A positive NPV means the project is expected to add value at the given discount rate.

Section B: Multiple Choice (30 questions)

1. Which of the following is an asset?

A. Accounts payable

B. Bank loan

C. Capital

D. Inventory

Answer: D. Inventory is an asset. Accounts payable and bank loan are liabilities, and capital is equity.

2. Which account normally has a debit balance?

A. Sales revenue

B. Accounts payable

C. Rent expense

D. Capital

Answer: C. Expenses have debit balances. Sales revenue, accounts payable and capital normally have credit balances.

3. What is the double-entry bookkeeping principle?

A. Every transaction affects only one account.

B. Every transaction has equal debit and credit entries.

C. Every transaction increases assets.

D. Every transaction must involve cash.

Answer: B. Double entry means every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits.

4. Which document is commonly issued when goods are sold on credit?

A. Purchase order

B. Sales invoice

C. Bank statement

D. Remittance advice

Answer: B. A sales invoice is issued to the customer when goods are sold on credit.

5. What is the purpose of a trial balance?

A. To calculate gross profit only

B. To identify all accounting errors

C. To check whether total debits equal total credits

D. To calculate cash sales

Answer: C. A trial balance checks that total debit balances equal total credit balances. It does not find every error.

6. Which financial statement shows the financial position of a business at a particular date?

A. Income statement

B. Statement of financial position

C. Cash flow statement

D. Statement of changes in production

Answer: B. The statement of financial position shows assets, liabilities and equity at a specific date.

7. Which statement best describes depreciation in accounting?

A. The process of increasing the value of a non-current asset

B. The systematic allocation of an asset's depreciable amount over its useful life

C. The process of recording the purchase of an asset as an immediate expense

D. The calculation of profit earned from selling a non-current asset

Answer: B. Depreciation spreads the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life.

8. Which of the following is a current liability?

A. Land

B. Trade receivables

C. Trade payables

D. Machinery

Answer: C. Trade payables are due within a year, so they are current liabilities. Land and machinery are non-current assets; trade receivables are current assets.

9. A business purchases equipment for cash. What is the immediate effect on total assets, assuming no other changes?

A. Total assets increase.

B. Total assets decrease.

C. Total assets remain unchanged.

D. Total liabilities increase.

Answer: C. Cash falls and equipment rises by the same amount, so total assets are unchanged.

10. How is gross profit calculated?

A. Total revenue minus operating expenses

B. Total assets minus total liabilities

C. Sales revenue minus cost of goods sold

D. Total cash receipts minus total cash payments

Answer: C. Gross profit = sales revenue minus cost of goods sold.

11. Which account is credited when a business makes a cash sale of goods?

A. Cash

B. Purchases

C. Sales

D. Rent expense

Answer: C. Cash is debited and sales is credited on a cash sale.

12. A business has assets of $80,000 and liabilities of $30,000. What is its capital?

A. $30,000

B. $50,000

C. $80,000

D. $110,000

Answer: B. Capital = assets minus liabilities = $80,000 - $30,000 = $50,000.

13. What is the main purpose of management accounting?

A. To prepare information exclusively for external investors

B. To support internal planning, control and decision-making

C. To calculate taxes only

D. To prepare statutory financial statements only

Answer: B. Management accounting supports internal planning, control and decision-making.

14. A product sells for $50 per unit, and its variable cost is $30 per unit. What is the contribution per unit?

A. $10

B. $20

C. $30

D. $80

Answer: B. Contribution per unit = $50 - $30 = $20.

15. A business has fixed costs of $12,000 and a contribution of $6 per unit. What is the break-even point in units?

A. 1,000 units

B. 1,500 units

C. 2,000 units

D. 3,000 units

Answer: C. Break-even units = fixed costs / contribution per unit = $12,000 / $6 = 2,000 units.

16. Which formula is used to calculate the margin of safety in units?

A. Break-even sales - Actual sales

B. Actual sales - Break-even sales

C. Fixed costs / Selling price

D. Variable costs / Contribution

Answer: B. Margin of safety = actual sales minus break-even sales.

17. Which of the following is a non-financial performance measure?

A. Gross profit margin

B. Return on capital employed

C. Customer satisfaction level

D. Operating profit

Answer: C. Customer satisfaction is non-financial. The others are financial measures.

18. Which technique evaluates the financial attractiveness of an investment by discounting future cash flows to their present value?

A. Break-even analysis

B. Absorption costing

C. Net Present Value (NPV)

D. Material price variance analysis

Answer: C. NPV discounts future cash flows to present value and deducts the initial investment.

19. What is the effect of purchasing inventory on credit?

A. Assets increase and liabilities increase.

B. Assets decrease and equity increases.

C. Liabilities decrease and expenses decrease.

D. Assets and liabilities both decrease.

Answer: A. Inventory (asset) rises and accounts payable (liability) rises.

20. Which of the following is classified as an expense?

A. Bank loan received

B. Owner's capital introduced

C. Electricity consumed by the business

D. Cash received from a customer for an existing receivable

Answer: C. Electricity consumed is an expense. The others are a liability, capital, and a settlement of a receivable.

21. A business has revenue of $25,000 and cost of sales of $15,000. What is its gross profit?

A. $5,000

B. $10,000

C. $15,000

D. $40,000

Answer: B. Gross profit = $25,000 - $15,000 = $10,000.

22. Which account is debited when a business pays rent in cash?

A. Cash account

B. Rent expense account

C. Sales revenue account

D. Capital account

Answer: B. Rent expense is debited and cash is credited.

23. What is the main purpose of an income statement?

A. To show the financial position at a specific date

B. To calculate profit or loss for an accounting period

C. To record all cash transactions

D. To list all assets and liabilities

Answer: B. The income statement calculates profit or loss for an accounting period.

24. What is the main purpose of a statement of financial position?

A. To calculate the net profit of a business

B. To show the assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific date

C. To record daily cash transactions

D. To calculate total sales revenue

Answer: B. It shows assets, liabilities and equity at a specific date.

25. What is the main purpose of preparing a trial balance?

A. To calculate the net profit of a business

B. To check whether total debit balances equal total credit balances

C. To determine the market value of assets

D. To calculate total cash sales

Answer: B. A trial balance checks that total debit balances equal total credit balances.

26. Which investment appraisal technique considers the time value of money by discounting future cash flows?

A. Payback period

B. Accounting rate of return (ARR)

C. Net present value (NPV)

D. Gross profit margin

Answer: C. NPV discounts future cash flows; payback and ARR do not use discounting.

27. A project requires an initial investment of $20,000 and generates annual cash inflows of $5,000. What is the payback period, assuming equal annual cash inflows?

A. 2 years

B. 3 years

C. 4 years

D. 5 years

Answer: C. Payback = $20,000 / $5,000 = 4 years.

28. What does a positive Net Present Value (NPV) suggest about an investment project?

A. The present value of cash inflows is less than the initial investment.

B. The project will generate no future cash flows.

C. The present value of expected cash inflows exceeds the present value of cash outflows.

D. The project will result in a financial loss.

Answer: C. A positive NPV means discounted inflows exceed discounted outflows, so the project adds value.

29. How is the accounting rate of return (ARR) commonly calculated?

A. Average annual accounting profit divided by average investment, multiplied by 100

B. Initial investment divided by annual cash inflow

C. Total revenue divided by total expenses

D. Annual cash inflow divided by payback period

Answer: A. ARR = (average annual accounting profit / average investment) x 100.

30. Why is effective working capital management important for a business?

A. To eliminate all operating expenses

B. To ensure the business can meet its short-term financial obligations

C. To increase non-current assets only

D. To avoid preparing financial statements

Answer: B. Good working capital management keeps the business liquid enough to meet short-term obligations.
